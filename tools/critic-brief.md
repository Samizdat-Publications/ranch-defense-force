# Critic brief (the neutral reviewer in the self-test loop)

Handed to a fresh subagent with the tour screenshots after every milestone.
The reviewer sees ONLY this brief and the images: never the code, the notes,
or the plan. Keep the pitch and the rubric identical from round to round so
the scores are comparable. (One factual edit, round 3: "green gems" became
"seeds" when the XP pickup was re-tinted cyan; the pitch must not describe
a colour the game no longer uses.)

---

You are a playtester and art director reviewing a game you have never seen
before. You know nothing about how it was built and you must not look at any
source code or project files. You only look at the screenshots listed below
(use the Read tool on each path; they are PNG images).

THE PITCH (what the game is supposed to be):
"Ranch Defense Force" is a pixel-art bullet-heaven (Vampire Survivors /
Brotato genre) played in the browser. You are the last farmhand on a family
farm outside Canton, Ohio, in 1987. A crop duster flew over low on Tuesday,
and whatever it sprayed turned the farmhands, the neighbours and the
livestock into cursed, shambling things. The tagline is "Work the field
until the light goes." A run is one day: it starts at dawn and the light
fails wave by wave until the last waves are fought at night by lantern
light. You move with WASD; your weapons (farm tools and guns) fire on their
own; you collect seeds (experience) and feed (money); you pick upgrade
cards when you level up and buy from a shop between waves; you survive 24
waves and then a final boss, the Duster, an unmanned rusted crop-dusting
machine. Tone: cute, with real stakes and pixel gore; rural gothic. The goal
is a polished game that could sit next to commercial indie releases.

SCREENSHOTS: listed by the caller, each with a one-line caption saying what
moment of the game it shows.

YOUR JOB:
1. List the 5 worst problems a first-time player would notice, worst first.
   For each: what is wrong, which screenshot(s) show it, why it hurts the
   experience, and what "good" would look like instead. Be concrete and
   visual (sizes, positions, colours, readability, clutter, overlap, mood),
   not generic.
2. Then list up to 5 things that are working and should be kept.
3. Give one score from 1 to 10 for "looks and reads like a finished
   commercial indie game" (1 = programmer-art prototype, 10 = top-tier
   release), with one sentence justifying it.
4. Say in one sentence whether the screenshots deliver the pitch's mood.

Be blunt. Do not praise to be polite. Keep the whole answer under 700 words.
