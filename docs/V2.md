# Ranch Defense Force v2: The Long Day

Started 2026-09-25 on branch `v2`. The v1 game is tagged `v1-final` (commit
`cc0c8d00`) and stays live on `main` until v2 beats it.

## Pitch

A pixel-art bullet-heaven in the browser. You are the last farmhand on the
Whitacre place outside Canton, Ohio, 1987. A crop duster went over low on
Tuesday, and whatever it sprayed turned the hands, the neighbours and the stock
into cursed, shambling things. **Work the field until the light goes.**

A run is **one day**. Wave 1 is first light; the last wave is full dark and the
Duster comes in with its lamps on. The sun crosses the sky, shadows swing and
lengthen, the light goes gold and then red, and by the end you are fighting by
lantern light with eyes shining in the corn. The blight spreads across the
ground as the day goes. The light is the clock, and the clock is the difficulty.

Controls: WASD / arrows / left stick to move, Space / right trigger for the
class ability, Esc to pause. Weapons fire on their own.

## What v1 got right and wrong (audit, 2026-09-25)

Baseline critic score: **4/10** ("the menus are close to commercial quality,
but the gameplay, which is what a player looks at 95% of the time, reads as a
prototype").

Keep:
- **The sim.** Fixed-step, pooled, deterministic, about 0.1 ms per tick, with
  bot pilots and a real test suite. Six classes, 164 cards, bosses, maps, meta.
- **The art library.** About 1000 generated objects and 28 characters with
  8-direction clips. Good pixel art, badly presented.
- **The card language** (hanging tags, rarity ribbons, written-out deltas) and
  the voice of the writing.

Replace:
- **The renderer.** Canvas 2D cannot do light, shadow, bloom or grading, and
  the premise is about light. The field was one flat midday green at every wave.
- **Scale.** Every animal was drawn on the human 32x64 grid, so a hen stood as
  tall as a man. The sim already gave them small radii; only the art was wrong.
- **The ground.** Wang transitions read as staircases; the field had no place.
- **The HUD.** Weapon slots covered the bottom of the play area, the XP bar was
  unlabelled, the level was nowhere.
- **Process weight.** A 5,700-line NOTES file, 86 tool scripts, docs that
  contradict each other, a 20-minute test suite. v1 notes are archived.

## Decisions log

**D1. Stay in TypeScript in the browser; replace Canvas 2D with WebGL2.**
Godot was considered seriously and rejected for this game:
- The target is the browser (GitHub Pages). Godot's web export ships a
  multi-megabyte wasm runtime and must use its Compatibility renderer, which is
  the reduced feature set.
- A bullet-heaven's hot loop is hundreds of entities steering, colliding and
  firing every tick. In V8 this sim runs at about 0.1 ms per tick. GDScript
  compiled to wasm would be several times slower, and C# does not export to web.
- The deterministic sim, its tests and its bot pilots are the most valuable code
  in the repo. A port would rewrite all of it for no player-visible gain.
- The self-test loop (drive the real game, photograph it, hand the photos to a
  critic) is native in a browser and awkward in Godot.
What Godot would have bought is lights, shaders, particles and post-processing.
The new renderer provides exactly those, without a dependency.

**D2. Pixel model: 1 art pixel = 1 buffer pixel.** The world renders into a
low-resolution buffer about 540 px tall (width follows the aspect ratio) and is
upscaled with a sharp-bilinear filter, so pixels stay crisp at any window size
and every sprite shares one pixel density. The camera's sub-pixel offset is
applied at upscale, so motion stays smooth without pixel crawl. Light, bloom
and fog are computed at buffer resolution and upsampled smoothly.

**D3. One scale for the cast.** Each sheet gets a `fieldScale` applied offline
in the atlas build: box filter, then snap to the sprite's own palette. Tested
on hens, dogs, sheep, crows and hogs at 0.42 to 0.75, and it holds up.

**D4. The day is the clock.** Time of day follows wave progress. Ambient
colour, sun angle, shadow length and grading are all driven from one curve in
content, so a designer can move dusk without touching code.

**D5. Readability at night is non-negotiable.** Enemies get a 1 px dark
outline from the sprite shader, and eyes and projectiles are emissive. A
darkened screen that hides what is killing you is a bug, not atmosphere.

**D6. Nothing in the bottom-centre of the play area.** The HUD hugs the edges.

**D7. The self-test loop.** `npm run tour` boots the real game in Chrome,
fast-forwards deterministic scenarios with a bot pilot, and photographs them
from the player's camera. After every milestone a critic subagent that sees
only the photos and this pitch lists the five worst problems, and they get
fixed until the worst remaining one is cosmetic.

**D8. The title is a live diorama, not a CSS illustration.** The game's own
renderer draws the Home Field at sundown behind the title; the six classes
stand round a burning barrel; every thirteen seconds lightning cuts to full
dark and the turned stand where they stood (the owner's idea from the v1
notes). The v1 home screen (three CSS scenes, a descent into a lab, about
3,000 lines) is no longer mounted. What survived from it: the class cards.

**D9. Sound for the time of day is synthesised.** Birds at first light,
wind all day, crickets from golden hour, an owl after dark, thunder on the
lightning. Nothing to source or license, and it follows the same day curve
as the picture.

**D10. Every surface map is a place.** A map without a `place` block would
still look like v1, and the seed picks the Home Field only a third of the
time. The other four got their own backdrops and ground; this is pure
content and cannot move a seed.

**D11. The dev overlay is opt-in** (`?dev` or F1). Screenshots and players
see the game, not the gauges.

**D12. The docs were cut back.** The six root-level v1 briefs and handoffs
contradicted each other and the code. They are archived under
`docs/archive/v1/`; the root now holds README, CLAUDE.md, NOTES.md and
ASSETS.md, each short and current.

**D13. Balance is left where v1 tuned it, pending a human.** The sim is
unchanged. v1 tuned it against bot pilots into a band the tests enforce (a
kiting bot clears a minority of seeds, an idle one almost never, every class
viable) after the owner's own playtest notes ("waves 1-5 need double the
enemies", done in v1). A stronger autopilot surviving longer in the photo
tour is not evidence against that band. Moving numbers without a human
playing is how v1 spent sessions tuning to its bots, so v2 changes what the
player sees and hears, and leaves what kills them alone until the owner
plays it.

**D14. The tour photographs the game a person plays.** Round 2 judged a
field carpeted in uncollected seeds, a player pinned to a fence and a health
bar that never moved. All three were the bot, not the game: the autopilot
only ever ran away. It now walks into its pickups when nothing is about to
hit it and drifts back toward the middle, and the tour stages health for the
late shots (`rdf.stageHp`), the same way it stages the hour. The autopilot
is a tour instrument only (no test reads it), so none of this touches D13.

**D15. The curse is drawn, not simulated.** Every non-boss enemy is pushed
toward a cold, drained pallor in the sprite shader, and its eyes (bright
yellow or red pixels) glow at every hour, not only after dark. Bosses keep
their colours. The XP seed is re-tinted cyan, the one hue the field, the gas
and the acid never use. Numbers in `tuning.render`.

**D16. D13 is lifted: the owner handed over balance too (2026-09-26).** In the
owner's words: anything that changes balance is fine, use all the assets, have the
reviewer make the suggestions and follow them; "more horde is also what I feel
it needs". Everything below follows from that. The tests still hold the same
SHAPE (an idle bot never clears, a kiting bot clears a minority, no class is a
trap), re-measured against the new game rather than loosened.

**D17. The horde.** The probe gained an on-screen count, and it showed why nine
reviewers saw no crowd: 6 to 17 enemies were ever on screen, because they
spawned on the fence of an arena ten screens big and died on arrival. Now:
most enemies step on from just past the edge of the screen all round the
player (`waves.json` spawn.ring); a `horde` block multiplies the threat
budget from 1.5x at dawn by 0.12x a wave (4.4x by the Duster; D24 raised it to 1.6x and 0.16x, 5.4x by the Duster) with groups
growing by its square root, and scales each ordinary enemy's hp (0.5 at
dawn, growing 0.024 a wave to 1.08 by the Duster), contact damage (0.6), xp
(0.45) and feed (0.15, after round 11 found 11,215 banked at the Duster). Seeds and feed dropped within
40 px of one already lying there merge into it and draw big. Measured on the
Hand: about 20 on screen at wave 1, 30 through the middle of the day, 45 to
50 in the last waves; home pilots clear a mean 2.5 of 8 runs, v1's band.

How the tests moved it. Health went 0.5 -> 0.42 -> 0.55: at 0.42 a wave-1
farmhand had 3 hp, every hit overkilled, and a bot taking random cards (more
weapons) beat one that merged (run.test.ts "merging beats taking whatever
came up": 101 waves against 128). At 0.55 merging wins again. Then
"a run nobody is playing" failed the other way: a Hand who never moved
cleared 3 of 24 (bar 2), 8 of 24 buying from the shop (bar 5), because the
ring delivers enemies to a stationary player and a flat late game let any
build alive at wave 12 cruise. A contact-damage ramp moved nothing; a health
ramp did: 0 and 5 of 24, with the home pilots still at 2.5 of 8. The Hand's
Braced now DRAINS over 0.75 s when he moves instead of resetting, because
a crowd arriving from every side made one step cost all of it and standing
measured no better than running.

**D18. A boss every third wave, all of them.** The four bosses the art pass
built and parked at weight 0 are in the schedule: the Cockerel (6), the
Thing in the North Pasture (9), the Prize Bull (12), the Sow (15), the
Combine (18), the Spray Rig (21) and the Duster (25). Base hp is set for the
wave each one lands on, because a boss's hp rides the wave's hp curve.

**D19. The Duster is the plane.** The story is a crop duster that flew over
low, and every reviewer expected the plane. The top-down plane already on
the account was given a rusted, cursed pass in PixelLab (one edit, about 20
generations) and flies: crop-dusting passes lane by lane that bank round in
arcs, then strafing runs through the player below half health, spraying
from both wing booms, with a drop shadow on the ground and a spiralling
crash. Round 10 found it the least visible thing at the climax, so it was
redrawn natively at 256 px (create_image_pro with the 128 px plane as
reference, about 40 generations over two tries; an edit only ever kept the
old size) and lights the field: a wide soft searchlight ahead, an engine
glow, and a moonlit rim of its own after dark. The rusted tractor that was the Duster is the Spray Rig at wave 21:
the same behaviour at tractor speed with no second phase, so it teaches the
lanes the plane will fly.

**D20. What was built and never switched on, is.** The crow (every surface
map's mix), the Bottoms (in rotation), the blighted wheat, rotten corn and
apple tree as harvestable nodes, the six foreground props (the title's near
band, with parallax), seven blighted hen breeds cycled through the infected
flock (hens and roosters from wave 1, so the opening is not one sprite), and
low crop rows planted in the worked fields among the crowd. The crystal ore nodes became farm salvage (milk cans, a
strongbox, a hand pump) from sheets already on the account.

**D21. Round 11, followed.** The owner asked for the reviewer's list to be
worked, so it was, item by item. Loose crops stand on a mound of turned soil
and sit back a shade (the art is the fruit alone, and on grass a lone tomato
read as a pickup); the Home Field lost its single cabbages, cauliflowers and
strawberries and a quarter of its crops, and its in-field rows sit back like
the loose ones. Feed pickups are cream, not orange, and the horde pays 0.15
of its feed where it paid 0.3. Only a crit draws its number in the big face.
The night outline is a dim dusk violet at a lower glow; the eyes carry the
dark. The Duster spins a propeller. The milk cans' white card is cut out
(`decard` passed it because its corners were transparent). The farmhand
wears three faces, two of them characters already on the account. Every
label the reviewer measured is 14 px or more, slots print their tier, and
the boss bar is 720 units wide.

**D22. Round 12, followed.** Feed the Birds paid 15% of kills a stack,
written for v1's crowd; four stacks under the horde was 9,000 feed a run and
the carpet of sacks the reviewer saw. It went to 4% a stack (D23 settles it at 12%). The player's own
clouds and pools draw at a quarter strength with a one-pixel edge, so they
stop reading as the Duster's poison; only blasts light the ground; one
ordinary damage number per 22x12 patch of screen. After dark the outline
is dark, as by day, and the bodies carry a little more pallor of their own:
the curse reads as pale shapes in dark lines, not ghosts. The Duster is faintly self-lit
and loses its hard shadow at night, which had read as a second plane, and
its searchlight starts ahead of the nose: thrown from under it, the beam
bleached its own wings white. Rounds glint after dark rather than blaze. The
worked fields lie flat under the fight. The labels are IBM Plex Mono: the
second pixel face in a row failed at label size. A hay wagon, a stone well
and three round bales stand inside the fence, and a kill leaves a mark one
time in fourteen instead of thirty.

**D23. Round 13, followed.** The day is a decline, not a switch: noon is
the brightest stop and every stop after it is darker and warmer (amber,
gold, then a red dusk with the vignette closing in at wave 17), and dawn is
pink-gold and under-exposed. Every cursed body carries blotches of chemical
green, faintly lit after dark: the spray's mark, shared by the whole cast.
Blood lands as a directional spray with a dark core, not a pool and two
flecks. The boss bar lives at the bottom centre, off the ground a boss is
framed on, and the Duster keeps a faint moon shadow so it reads airborne.
Nothing from the fight draws over a paused sheet. The weapon slots are 60
units on light tiles with the art at 2x. The primary button is gold on
every screen (a selector outranked it). The title's burn barrel lost its
baked smoke column, and its hero cards grew so no line is under 12 px.
Feed the Birds drops 12% a stack, merged into sacks already lying close:
at 4% the Hand's smart bot cleared 2 of 24 (the bar is 4), and paid straight
into the purse it cleared 3, because a token on the ground is also
something the pilots walk to.

**D24. Rounds 14 and 15, and more horde.** A sprite's glow output now
covers what stands behind it (the crowd's eyes had shone through the
Duster's wings), and the Duster is drawn rust-dark with a moon shadow and its
searchlight made of three soft pools (the cone left a white triangle). Gas
is soft, stepped density instead of an ordered dither; the player's own
pools are gold. Worked plots are rounded rectangles whose edges wander, so a
field is not a ruler-straight seam and the lantern does not light a box. The
grass lost its lavender flowers. The day's tints went back toward neutral:
two rounds in a row read strong tints as "a filter", so the hour is carried
by sun colour, shadow length and exposure. A merged seed draws as a small
pile of seeds (the big seed read as an egg). The elemental rounds got
cartridge icons in the house style; the cards got bigger type and lost the
tier-name repeat. Feed left at the end sells for acres (150 a piece). The
horde starts at 1.6 and climbs 0.16 a wave (5.4 by the Duster) with xp at
0.35 a kill, and every em dash in the repository, visible or not, is gone.

**D25. Round 16, and the Prize Bull at native scale.** The bull the
reviewers liked most was a 68 px sprite drawn at 2x; it is now a native
128 px PixelLab character (pro quadruped styled on the infected farmhand, v3
animations in eight directions), shifted per direction so its feet stand on
its position, drawn at 1x. The Spray Rig's tractor, already 110 px, is drawn
at 1x too. Ordinary damage numbers are off (crits only, a content switch);
acid pools are a faint wash inside a bright rim; fire and blasts glow rather
than floodlight; the dusk keeps its range. The Duster is a shade lighter,
sprays a thicker, faintly lit green, and the arrow to an off-screen boss is
self-lit so it survives the night. Plots bow on a longer wave and crop rows
end raggedly; grass tufts are sparser. The clock reads "WAVE 12/24 · 0:36",
the HUD's currency is the feed sack, and the results list shows a whole
build. Balance moved with the horde: merges multiply damage 1.7x a tier (was
1.6), because more, weaker bodies let the random-card bot outlast the merging
one; raising enemy health instead broke the Hand's reason to stand.

**D26. Round 17.** Enemy telegraphs are warnings rather than hitboxes: a
fill that thins toward the edge in bands, a pulsing rim, and chevrons
marching out along the line of the charge, with the straight side lines
gone. The player's outline-only pass (drawn over the crowd) carries its own
light, so the farmhand stays findable at any hour. The Duster's art had
transparent gaps between its wings that showed the well and the crowd
through the plane; the enclosed holes are filled with under-wing shade.
Shadows cap at 1.6 body lengths and thin as the sun sets. Two pacing
switches were built and left off, measured: a merge tier gate
(`tuning.merge.tierOpensAt`; every gate tried cost `run.test.ts` a bar)
and shop price inflation (`waves.json` economy.pricePerWave). Both are the
owner's call; see NOTES.md.

**D27. Round 18.** The player and everything he carries draw after the
depth-sorted crowd, so a zombie in front of him no longer hides him; tree
canopies still draw over him. The Duster is new art: a pixflux generation at
256 px, one art pixel per world pixel like the cast, replacing a plane that
was redrawn from a 128 px reference and kept its chunky pixels. Its moon rim
is dimmer and cooler, and the Duster's gas no longer casts its own light. A
seed on the ground is a small outlined diamond (`tuning.render.seed`), not
the 16 px gem the reviewer found "as big as a chicken"; dawn carries a
heavier warm mist; and the curse's pallor is cooler, blue-grey rather than
pink-lilac, so the crowd separates from the blighted ground of the
afternoon as well as from the grass.

**D28. Round 19.** The healthy grass tile measured one flat colour, so the
field was a smooth wash under crisp sprites; the ground shader draws blades
now, one art pixel wide with a lit tip, on two jittered grids so no period
shows. The fog is flat dithered wisps instead of rounded blotches, and blood
soaks in as dark maroon decals. Seeds and feed that lie ten seconds drift to
the player (`tuning.pickups.settleSeconds`; heal jars and gear stay put).
Every telegraph is one shape: a spray is its cone filling from the mouth as
the wind-up runs out, a charge is a body-wide lane exactly as long as the run
(`addTelegraph` takes a width), and the Duster and the spray rig lay their
lane out ahead of them (`tuning.render.sprayLane`). Warnings are self-lit so
the night cannot swallow them. After dusk every cursed body wears a
one-pixel moonlit rim on its top edge and its eyes burn hotter. The Duster
at night is a dark shape with a cool moon rim, red and green wingtip lamps
and a tail strobe, its searchlight thrown clear of its own wings. Ordinary
hits throw the weapon's own burst (`impactFx` on each weapon) unless an
element owns the look; the auger's swing is a ground shockwave, not the
demon-bite loop. A held tool rises to rest between thrusts. The win screen
has a picture: the Duster down in the corn and burning, the hand in the
lantern light, first light on the horizon, and the build as an icon grid.
Card keys are keycaps. (Level-up cards hung at their own length for one round; round 20 read that as uneven, and they are equal again.)

**D29. Round 20 (6/10).** The ground is calmer and a step darker (the
blades are sparse; a denser pass read as "fine grainy streaks"), the curse's
eyes are the spray's sick green, blood lands as a bigger pool with a wet
highlight, gas is a faint fill inside a bright ragged rim, the crit burst is
smaller, and the Duster at night is a shade lighter than black. Stat rows
compare before and after, so an improvement to a negative stat is green; the
day's sheet sets its numbers in the label face (the wood type's 1 read as an
i); a shop card says "Buy". The elemental rounds pack unconformed, because
the house palette has no red, blue or green tip to give them. Dawn is gold
under a cooler shade, mid-morning brighter, and the wave card sits in the
lower third. The tour stages the bull in shorter steps: the round-20 build
killed it between top-ups and the shot caught its death fade.

**D30. Round 21 (6/10).** Blood marks the ground only where something
died: the sim tags a death's blood (`BLOOD_KILL`, `BLOOD_BOSS`, exported from
content so sim and render share them) with the same random draws as before,
and the renderer pools one drop in `stainKeep` (24) of those, a boss's all of
them at twice the size; hit blood soaks in. Elites wear hot red, not the
player's gold. The pallor is lifted so the crowd sits above the field.
Explosions are self-lit and yellower; the everyday impact is smaller. The
dusk keeps its hue with more contrast. The Duster loses its outline-only moon
rim ("an editor selection"), keeps its rust and casts a stronger shadow.
Sacks dim after dark. The shop has a Buy button with the price and Hold
beside it, and plain category words (AMMO, MONEY). The tier on a HUD tile is
a coloured corner badge; the title's hero cards sit level. The boss bar stays
at the bottom: rounds 13 and 21 disagree about where it belongs, and round
13's reason (the top is where a boss is framed) still holds.

**D31. Round 22 (5/10).** The reason the day never showed: the
composite's knee, put there so a lantern would not bleach its holder,
squashed the whole illumination, sun included, so every daylight stop above 1
came out at about 1. It applies to the lamps only now, and the stops were
rewritten to carry four looks before dusk: dawn cold and misty under a pink
sun (the day fog is lavender), 7 AM gold with long shadows, 9 AM fresh, noon
bleached, then amber. Measured frame luma now runs 78, 103, 119, 87, 52, 34
from dawn to night (it was 71, 79, 92, 72, 51, 42). Blood pools come in three
shapes built from hashed lumps. The Duster's lamps are bigger and its engine
glows at the nose. The pallor is 0.65. The lose screen has a picture: the
hand face-down at the corn's edge, what took him standing over him, and
"Taken by the Farmhand on wave 1" (`world.hurtBy`, bookkeeping only). Body
ink is darker. The tour shoots wave 7 mid-wave, where the crowd is.
